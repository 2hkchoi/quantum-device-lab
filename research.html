Research

Controlling electrons one at a time—and revealing collective quantum behavior.

We combine gate-defined quantum devices, cryogenic transport, charge sensing, interferometry, and numerical modelling to study coherence, interactions, entropy, and electron dynamics.

Four connected themes

From quantum states to measurable signals.

Our research follows electrons from their coherent propagation and many-body interactions to the devices and measurement methods that make those phenomena accessible.

01 Coherence · Interference

Electron Quantum Optics

Electronic Mach–Zehnder and Fabry–Pérot interferometers turn quantum Hall edge channels into controllable matter-wave pathways. We use them to investigate coherence, dephasing, complementarity, and the way Coulomb interactions reshape interference.

  • How does which-path information suppress—and erase—electronic interference?
  • How do interactions modify phase, effective charge, and the area of a quantum Hall interferometer?
Representative work

Controlled Dephasing of an Electron Interferometer · PRL 109, 250401 (2012)

An electronic quantum eraser · Science 344, 1363 (2014)

Robust electron pairing in the integer quantum Hall effect regime · Nature Communications 6, 7435 (2015)

02 Entropy · Correlations

Quantum Dot Thermodynamics & Many-Body Physics

Quantum dots offer a tunable setting in which charge, spin, orbital degeneracy, and inter-dot coupling can be controlled one electron at a time. Charge sensing and reservoir thermometry connect these microscopic states to thermodynamic quantities such as entropy.

  • How do degeneracy and coupling alter the entropy of few-electron systems?
  • How can many-body correlations be identified through thermodynamic measurements?
Representative work & current direction

Charge Frustration in a Triangular Triple Quantum Dot · PRL 110, 046803 (2013)

Strong electron-occupancy dependence of the lever arm · JKPS 87, 67 (2025)

Direct entropy measurements in coupled quantum dots · Ongoing research

03 Modes · Energy relaxation

Coherent Electron Dynamics

Open electronic cavities, transverse magnetic focusing, and quantum-dot electron emitters let us resolve how electrons propagate, form spatial modes, retain phase information, and exchange energy with their environment.

  • Which electronic modes survive in an open, phase-coherent cavity?
  • How do injected hot electrons relax as they travel through a two-dimensional electron gas?
Representative work

Significant energy relaxation of quantum dot emitted hot electrons · PR Research 3, 033015 (2021)

Observation of Electronic Modes in Open Cavity Resonator · Nature Communications 14, 415 (2023)

Channel-resolved wavefunctions of transverse magnetic focusing · Communications Physics 7, 418 (2024)

04 Control · Instrumentation

Quantum Devices & Cryogenic Measurement

Reliable quantum experiments begin with robust electrostatic control and careful calibration. We develop gate-defined devices, cryogenic electronics, and measurement techniques for precise operation across demanding bias, frequency, and magnetic-field conditions.

  • How can quantum point contacts remain stable under high bias and magnetic field?
  • How can fast electrical signals and device energy scales be measured accurately at cryogenic temperatures?
Representative work

Experimental approach

A connected measurement platform.

Each project brings together device design, low-temperature experiments, quantitative calibration, and physical modelling.

01

Gate-defined devices

Quantum dots, point contacts, interferometers, and open cavities formed in high-mobility electron systems.

02

Cryogenic sensing

Low-noise transport, charge detection, thermometry, and high-frequency electrical measurements.

03

Quantum interference

Phase-sensitive transport and noise measurements that resolve coherence and interactions.

04

Numerical modelling

Electrostatic and quantum-transport calculations that connect device geometry to observed signals.
